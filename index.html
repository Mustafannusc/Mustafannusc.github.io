Short Bio

I am a computational materials scientist and nuclear engineering researcher specializing in atomistic, multiscale, and data-driven modeling of materials for extreme environments. My research focuses on understanding how atomic-scale defects, interfaces, irradiation, temperature, and mechanical loading influence the performance of advanced structural materials. I use molecular dynamics, atomistic simulation, first-principles-informed modeling, machine learning, and materials informatics to investigate irradiation damage, defect evolution, interfacial behavior, deformation, and structure–property relationships. My work spans oxide-dispersion-strengthened steels, high-entropy and refractory multi-principal-element alloys, SiC-based materials and interfaces, Ni-based alloys, nuclear fuel systems, and molten-salt/material interactions, with applications in nuclear energy, aerospace, additive manufacturing, and high-temperature technologies. I am particularly interested in integrating physics-based simulations with machine-learning interatomic potentials and predictive models to accelerate materials discovery and support the design of radiation-tolerant, thermally stable, and mechanically robust materials.

I received my Ph.D. in Nuclear Engineering and Technology and have held postdoctoral and visiting research appointments at Worcester Polytechnic Institute, Missouri University of Science and Technology, The University of Alabama, and Xi’an Jiaotong University. In parallel with my research activities, I have also contributed to engineering and physics education through adjunct teaching and teaching-assistant roles at Worcester Polytechnic Institute and Missouri S&T, supporting instruction in areas related to materials science, nuclear engineering, and engineering fundamentals.
