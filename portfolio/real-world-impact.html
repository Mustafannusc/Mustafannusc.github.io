From Atomistic Simulation to Engineering Decisions

My simulations are not the end product. They are a virtual materials laboratory used to connect atomic-scale mechanisms to measurable engineering properties, component reliability, manufacturing decisions, and material-selection questions in nuclear energy, aerospace, high-temperature systems, and additive manufacturing.

Engineering translation: atomic mechanism → property prediction → component-level risk or performance indicator → material/process decision → experimental or system-level validation.

1. SiC/PyC/SiC Interface Mechanics & Irradiation

Nuclear fuel systemsCeramic compositesFusion / fission

Real-world problem: SiC-based composite systems can experience interfacial damage, irradiation-induced defects, thermal stress, and loss of load transfer.

What I calculate: interfacial traction/separation, adhesion/debonding, contact evolution, indentation response, defect production and irradiation-driven structural change.

Engineering property represented: interface strength, damage tolerance, crack/debonding tendency, local mechanical response.

Decision supported: Which interface architecture and processing state is more likely to retain integrity under thermal-mechanical and irradiation loading?

Application context: SiC/SiC composites are leading accident-tolerant fuel-cladding candidates and are also studied for fusion structural/first-wall environments.

2. NiTi LPBF & Nanoparticle Consolidation

Additive manufacturingAerospaceBiomedicalActuators

Real-world problem: Laser powder-bed fusion must melt and consolidate feedstock without excessive porosity, poor bonding, composition loss, or undesirable phase evolution.

What I calculate: heating/melting behavior, time above melt, particle neck growth, wetting/contact, mixing width, Ni penetration, porosity-related descriptors, retained phase/order and cooling-rate descriptors.

Engineering property represented: consolidation quality, melt efficiency, interparticle bonding, process sensitivity and expected functional-material quality.

Decision supported: Which laser-energy/process conditions are worth testing experimentally, and which conditions are likely to produce weak or defective consolidation?

3. ODS Steels: Irradiation Damage & Nanomechanics

Reactor core materialsRadiation toleranceStructural alloys

Real-world problem: Reactor structural materials accumulate vacancies, interstitials and dislocation structures while operating under neutron irradiation and high temperature.

What I calculate: displacement-cascade damage, defect survival/clustering, oxide-particle and grain-boundary sink behavior, dislocation-obstacle interaction, nanoindentation response and diffusion.

Engineering property represented: irradiation hardening tendency, defect-removal efficiency, obstacle strengthening, microstructural stability and damage accumulation.

Decision supported: How should oxide size/distribution, interfaces and microstructure be engineered to improve strength while reducing long-term irradiation damage?

4. HEA / RMPEA Alloy Design

AerospaceNuclearPower generationExtreme temperature

Real-world problem: Conventional alloys lose strength or stability at extreme temperature, but the compositional search space of refractory multi-principal-element alloys is enormous.

What I calculate: composition-dependent structural/mechanical response, local order, deformation trends and candidate descriptors for screening and machine-learning models.

Engineering property represented: high-temperature strength potential, stability, deformation resistance and composition-property trends.

Decision supported: Which alloy compositions should move forward to CALPHAD/DFT refinement, melting, additive manufacturing and mechanical/oxidation testing?

5. 800H/Ni Interfaces: MD + ML

High-temperature reactorsHeat exchangersInterfacesML surrogate models

Real-world problem: High-temperature components can fail from irradiation damage, void evolution, diffusion and degradation of bonded or dissimilar-material interfaces.

What I calculate: irradiation-induced defect evolution, void shrinkage/evolution, interface mixing/diffusion and mechanical response, then connect simulation outputs to ML prediction.

Engineering property represented: interface integrity, radiation-damage sensitivity, strength retention and defect-driven mechanical degradation.

Decision supported: Which irradiation/interface conditions present the highest integrity risk, and where should expensive experiments or higher-scale models be concentrated?

Application context: Alloy 800H has a documented history in high-temperature gas-reactor heat exchangers and reactor-internal component studies. The modeled 800H/Ni interface is a research architecture used to study interface physics rather than a claim of a specific commercial joint.

How to Read My Computational Work

At the atomistic scale I do not simulate an entire reactor, turbine, implant, or printed component. I isolate the rate-controlling mechanism that governs failure or performance, such as a defect interacting with an oxide, an interface beginning to debond, a displacement cascade generating damage, atoms mixing across a bonded interface, or powder particles melting and forming a neck. Those mechanisms are converted into quantitative descriptors that can feed continuum models, process maps, machine-learning surrogates, experimental design and materials qualification.

This is the role of atomistic and multiscale modeling in engineering: reduce expensive trial-and-error by identifying mechanisms, ranking candidate conditions, and predicting where testing effort should be concentrated.

Application-context note: The component/application mappings on this page are based on established literature and national-laboratory usage contexts for SiC/SiC cladding, high-temperature Alloy 800H, ODS steels, refractory HEAs and additively manufactured NiTi. They are presented as engineering relevance and candidate-use contexts, not as claims that every portfolio model is a certified or deployed component.
