Understanding Molten-Salt Corrosion Mechanisms with MLIP-Enabled Molecular Dynamics

Machine-learning interatomic-potential simulations of molten fluoride salts, oxide additives, ceramic interfaces, transport, and corrosion-related interfacial processes for advanced nuclear-energy systems.

← Back to Research Portfolio
Research objective: use Matlantis/PFP machine-learning interatomic potentials together with molecular dynamics to connect molten-salt chemistry, thermophysical behavior, ion transport, oxide additives, and salt/material interfacial structure to corrosion-relevant mechanisms.

Scientific Basis from My Molten-Salt Publications

This project builds on my published and ongoing work on the CaF2-MgF2 fluoride system and salt/ceramic interfaces. The published studies establish the structural, thermodynamic, transport, thermophysical, and interfacial-corrosion questions that motivate the current MLIP-enabled simulation workflow.

MLIP / Matlantis Simulation Strategy

1. Molten-salt structure: construct and equilibrate CaF2-MgF2 compositions and oxide-modified systems over relevant high-temperature conditions.
2. MLIP molecular dynamics: use Matlantis/PFP to access longer trajectories and larger chemically complex systems than conventional first-principles molecular dynamics can usually accommodate.
3. Thermophysical and transport analysis: extract density, radial distribution functions, coordination environments, diffusion coefficients, ionic mobility, and composition-dependent transport descriptors.
4. Salt/material interfaces: construct molten-salt/YSZ and related structural-material interfaces to examine adsorption, interfacial ordering, ion accumulation, oxygen-containing species, and local chemical rearrangement.
5. Corrosion descriptors: track species migration, coordination changes, dissolution-related events, interface reconstruction, concentration profiles, and temperature/composition trends that can be compared with experiment or higher-scale corrosion models.

Quantitative Outputs

Structure

Radial distribution functions, coordination numbers, local structural motifs, clustering, and composition-dependent ordering in the liquid.

Transport

Mean-squared displacement, self-diffusion coefficients, ionic mobility, and temperature-dependent transport behavior.

Thermophysical Response

Density and other temperature/composition-sensitive properties used to connect atomistic structure with engineering-scale salt behavior.

Corrosion-Relevant Interfaces

Interfacial composition profiles, adsorption, oxygen/oxide effects, atomic rearrangement, local disorder, and species exchange near YSZ or structural-material surfaces.

Engineering Relevance

Molten fluoride salts are important in advanced nuclear-energy concepts because their chemistry, transport properties, and interaction with containment or ceramic materials directly affect materials compatibility. A validated MLIP workflow can provide atomistic descriptors for screening salt chemistry, oxide additives, operating temperature, and candidate interface materials before more expensive experimental campaigns.
