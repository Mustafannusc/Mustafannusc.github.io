What My Research Does

I use atomistic simulation, multiscale modeling, and machine learning to answer engineering questions before expensive materials are manufactured, irradiated, tested, or placed into service.

The goal is not simply to visualize atoms. The goal is to identify the mechanisms that control failure or performance, convert them into measurable engineering metrics, and use those predictions to guide material selection, alloy design, interface design, manufacturing conditions, experiments, prototypes, and eventual qualification.

From Atomic-Scale Physics to an Industrial Decision

1. Real Problemradiation, heat, stress, corrosion, laser processing
→
2. Atomic Mechanismdefects, diffusion, interfaces, dislocations, melting, phase change
→
3. Predicted Metricstrength, adhesion, defect density, stability, mixing, process window
→
4. Engineering Decisionwhich material, composition, interface, or process condition to test next
5. Targeted Experimentmechanical, thermal, irradiation, microscopy, AM process test
→

→
7. Scale-Up & Qualificationlarger-scale tests, standards, reliability, lifetime and deployment
Engineering value: reduce trial-and-error, reduce expensive experiments, identify failure mechanisms earlier, rank candidate materials and processing conditions, and focus validation effort where it has the highest value.

How My Main Research Projects Translate

SiC/PyC/SiC Interfaces

Real problem: ceramic-composite interfaces in nuclear and high-temperature environments must retain load transfer while resisting irradiation damage and debonding.

I predict: adhesion, traction-separation behavior, contact evolution, defect production, indentation response and failure initiation.

Engineering use: guide interface architecture, material processing, irradiation testing and damage-tolerance assessment.

ODS Steels

Real problem: reactor structural alloys must tolerate neutron damage while maintaining high-temperature strength.

I predict: defect production and survival, oxide/dislocation interaction, grain-boundary sink behavior, diffusion and irradiation-related strengthening.

Engineering use: guide oxide size/distribution and microstructural design for radiation-tolerant structural materials.

HEA / RMPEA Alloy Design

Real problem: nuclear, aerospace and power-generation systems need alloys that remain strong and stable at extreme temperature.

I predict: composition-property trends, local order, deformation response and descriptors for ML/CALPHAD/DFT screening.

Engineering use: reduce the composition search space before alloy melting, AM processing, oxidation testing and mechanical qualification.

800H/Ni Interfaces: MD + ML

Real problem: high-temperature material systems can lose integrity through irradiation defects, void evolution, diffusion and interface degradation.

I predict: defect/void evolution, interface mixing, diffusion and mechanical response, then use ML to accelerate prediction across conditions.

Engineering use: identify high-risk conditions and prioritize the most informative experiments and higher-scale models.

NiTi LPBF & Nanoparticle Consolidation

Real problem: laser additive manufacturing must produce sufficient melting and bonding without excessive defects or loss of functional phase behavior.

I predict: melting, time above melt, neck growth, wetting, mixing, penetration, thermal history and process descriptors.

Engineering use: identify promising laser/process windows before expensive printing campaigns and prototype testing.
