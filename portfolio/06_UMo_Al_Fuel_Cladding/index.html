UMo-Al Monolithic Fuel and Cladding-Interface Modeling

Atomistic investigation of HIP-bonded AA6061 cladding, diffusion-barrier interfaces, residual-stress mechanisms, and delamination risk in U-Mo monolithic research-reactor fuel plates.

← Back to Research Portfolio
Engineering objective: connect interface chemistry, diffusion, defects, oxide/precipitate structure, thermal history, and mechanical loading to cladding bond strength and the initiation of cladding-cladding or fuel-cladding separation.

Real Fuel-System Context

Low-enriched U-Mo monolithic fuel concepts use a U-Mo fuel foil together with a Zr diffusion barrier and AA6061 cladding. Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) bonds the plate assembly. The interface region is therefore not merely a geometric boundary: its residual stress, local chemistry, oxide condition, precipitates, diffusion behavior, and irradiation response can affect plate integrity.

Fuel Plate Architecture

U-Mo fuel meat → Zr diffusion barrier → AA6061 cladding → HIP consolidation.

Component question: can the bonded plate retain structural integrity through fabrication, handling, irradiation, and thermal-mechanical service?

Failure Mechanisms of Interest

Cladding-cladding separation, interfacial delamination, residual stress from HIP, oxide/precipitate-controlled bonding, interdiffusion, post-irradiation degradation, and local mechanical-property mismatch.

Current Atomistic Modeling Scope

Stage 1: AA6061 / AA6061 HIP interface

The first reproducible model focuses on the cladding-cladding interface because it can be treated with a validated multicomponent Al-Si-Mg-Cu-Fe interaction model before introducing the much more demanding U-Mo-Zr-Al cross-interaction problem.

Model build: construct two AA6061 slabs with controlled composition, orientation, surface separation, and optional non-diffusible/interface constraints.
HIP thermal history: energy minimization → thermal ramp → hold near the literature HIP temperature range → cooling/relaxation.
Mechanical interrogation: tensile separation or normal loading across the bonded interface to obtain traction, peak stress, separation, and work-of-separation trends.
Transport/interfacial analysis: species-resolved density profiles, mean-squared displacement, mixing width, local coordination/structure, void formation, and diffusion near the interface.

Connection to Experiment

Published characterization of HIP-bonded AA6061/AA6061 interfaces reports Mg-Si-rich precipitates, MgO-related nanoscale features, Fe-rich phases, oxide-thickness effects, and a strong dependence of bond quality on HIP processing conditions. The simulation program is designed to test which atomic-scale mechanisms could explain those experimentally observed trends and to identify quantities that can be compared with mechanical testing and microscopy.

Planned Quantitative Outputs

Interface Mechanics

Traction-separation curve, peak interface stress, critical opening displacement, work of separation, local stress concentration, and defect nucleation.

Thermal / Diffusion Response

Temperature-dependent diffusion, mixing width, species redistribution, thermal expansion, and interface structural stability.

Microstructure Descriptors

Local atomic structure, coordination, dislocation/defect evolution, free-volume/void indicators, and concentration profiles across the interface.

Engineering Translation

Identify conditions that strengthen or weaken the HIP bond and prioritize fabrication temperatures, interface states, and validation tests.

Future Model Extensions

After the AA6061/AA6061 baseline is validated, the project can progress toward oxide-containing cladding interfaces, Zr/Al interdiffusion, U-Mo/Zr/Al layered models, irradiation-defect interactions, and coupling atomistic outputs to continuum residual-stress or fuel-performance models.

Potential-selection requirement: the Al-Si-Mg-Cu-Fe MEAM model can support the metallic AA6061 baseline only if the exact parameter set is validated for the target properties. An Al2O3-containing interface requires an interaction model that explicitly and consistently includes oxygen. A full U-Mo-Zr-Al model requires validated cross interactions for those species. These extensions should not be created by mixing unrelated potentials without verification.
