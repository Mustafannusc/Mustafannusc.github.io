Irradiated 800H/Ni Interfaces: Molecular Dynamics + Machine Learning

Physics-informed integration of irradiation-damage simulations, interfacial nanovoid evolution, tensile response, feature engineering, and machine-learning prediction.

← Back to Research Portfolio

Engineering question: How do irradiation-induced defect retention and residual void morphology at 800H/Ni interfaces control post-irradiation stiffness and strength, and can MD-derived descriptors predict those properties efficiently with machine learning?

Project Summary

Molecular-dynamics simulations were used to investigate displacement-cascade evolution and post-irradiation tensile behavior in 800H/Ni interfaces containing interfacial nanovoids. The resulting simulation data were converted into physics-informed descriptors and used to train machine-learning models for prediction of Young's modulus and ultimate tensile strength. The combined MD–ML workflow links irradiation-driven microstructural evolution to mechanical-property degradation and enables rapid comparison across interface and void configurations.

My Contribution

  • Performed MD simulations of displacement-cascade evolution in 800H/Ni interfaces containing interfacial nanovoids.
  • Quantified defect retention, void stability, residual void morphology, and post-irradiation microstructural evolution.
  • Simulated tensile response and extracted Young's modulus and ultimate tensile strength.
  • Constructed MD-derived feature vectors linking irradiation conditions, defect metrics, density changes, damage level, and residual void morphology to mechanical response.
  • Trained and compared regression models for prediction and feature-importance interpretation.

Methods & Tools

  • LAMMPS molecular dynamics
  • Primary knock-on atom / displacement-cascade simulations
  • Post-irradiation tensile deformation
  • Defect and void-evolution analysis
  • Python / scikit-learn
  • Random Forest, XGBoost, Support Vector Regression, and linear regression
  • Correlation, feature-importance, and model-validation analysis
  • OVITO atomistic visualization

Key Findings Represented in the Published Study

  • Irradiation-induced defect retention is asymmetric across the 800H/Ni interface, with greater residual damage on the 800H side.
  • Void stability depends strongly on initial void size under displacement cascades.
  • Residual void morphology is closely connected to post-irradiation tensile behavior.
  • Machine-learning models using MD-derived descriptors predict Young's modulus and ultimate tensile strength and identify influential microstructural features.
Project value: Demonstrates a complete physics-informed MD–ML workflow that converts irradiation-induced microstructural descriptors into rapid predictions of post-irradiation mechanical performance.
