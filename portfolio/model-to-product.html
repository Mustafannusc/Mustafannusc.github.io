From Modeling Prediction to Industrial Product

The goal is not to stop at simulation. The computational work defines mechanisms, narrows the design space, identifies measurable targets, and guides the next experiment. The same information can then support proof-of-concept prototypes, scale-up studies, qualification testing, and ultimately component or process decisions.

Engineering development chain: atomic-scale physics → predictive model → targeted experiment → prototype / proof of concept → larger-scale validation → qualification and industrial deployment.
1

Physics-Based Modeling

  • MD / DFT / CALPHAD
  • Defects, interfaces, diffusion
  • Mechanical and thermal mechanisms
  • Irradiation or laser-process response

Output: mechanistic descriptors

2

Prediction & ML

  • Property prediction
  • Surrogate models
  • Design-space screening
  • Uncertainty / sensitivity

Output: ranked conditions & candidates

3

Targeted Experiments

  • Choose composition/process windows
  • Mechanical testing
  • Microscopy / phase analysis
  • Irradiation or thermal exposure

Output: validation data

4

Prototype / Proof of Concept

  • Small coupon or demonstrator
  • AM build or joined interface
  • Functional testing
  • Compare model ↔ experiment

Output: demonstrated feasibility

5

Scale-Up Design

  • Larger geometry
  • Process repeatability
  • Component-level FEM / multiphysics
  • Manufacturing constraints

Output: scale-up process & component design

6

Qualification & Product

  • Standards and acceptance criteria
  • Lifetime / reliability assessment
  • Cost and manufacturability
  • Industry deployment

Output: qualified material/process/product

How My Portfolio Fits This Pathway

SiC/PyC/SiC Interfaces

Model: adhesion, debonding, irradiation defects and contact evolution.

Next experiment: interface-strength testing, microscopy of irradiated interfaces, thermal-mechanical cycling.

Prototype direction: optimized SiC-based composite interface architecture for cladding or high-temperature CMC applications.

ODS Steels

Model: defect sinks, cascade damage, dislocation-oxide interactions and strengthening mechanisms.

Next experiment: ion/neutron irradiation, nanoindentation, TEM defect characterization, tensile/creep testing.

Prototype direction: candidate ODS microstructures with optimized oxide dispersion for radiation-tolerant structural components.

HEA / RMPEA

Model: composition-property relationships, local structure and high-temperature deformation trends.

Next experiment: alloy synthesis, XRD/SEM, oxidation, tensile/compression and creep testing.

Prototype direction: down-selected extreme-environment alloy compositions for aerospace, nuclear and power applications.

800H/Ni + ML

Model: irradiation defects, diffusion, interface mixing, void evolution and mechanical response.

Next experiment: diffusion couples, interface tensile/shear testing, irradiation and microscopy.

Prototype direction: optimized dissimilar-material interface or joining strategy for high-temperature systems.

NiTi LPBF

Model: laser heating, melting, neck growth, mixing and process descriptors.

Next experiment: parameter-matrix LPBF builds, density/porosity analysis, DSC/XRD, tensile and shape-memory testing.

Prototype direction: validated LPBF process window for functional NiTi components and subsequent component-scale builds.

What Makes the Computational Stage Valuable

Experiments become much more expensive as the project moves toward larger samples, irradiation campaigns, advanced microscopy, prototype fabrication and qualification. The computational stage is therefore used as an engineering filter: it identifies the mechanisms that matter, removes poor candidates early, defines measurable hypotheses, and concentrates experimental resources on the most informative compositions, interfaces and processing conditions.

This creates a closed design loop: model → predict → test → update → prototype → scale → qualify. The final objective is a material, process window, interface architecture or component design that can be manufactured and validated at engineering scale.
